# Premium literary image style

Use this direction for calm Douyin book videos built from full-screen landscapes, period environments, illustrations, and reflective narration.

## Preserve from the reference structure

- Fill the 9:16 canvas with one strong image rather than placing it inside a card.
- Use slow, almost imperceptible camera movement.
- Keep captions to one or two short lines and highlight no more than one phrase.
- Allow images to breathe; avoid changing shots for every subtitle.
- Use a restrained warm accent instead of multiple bright colors.
- Let narration lead and keep music atmospheric.

## Upgrade the typography

Create three distinct text roles:

1. **Publishing title** — expressive Chinese display type or custom-composed title, large enough to read as identity rather than a watermark.
2. **Narrative captions** — highly legible Song/Ming serif or refined humanist sans, with careful line length, punctuation, and optical centering.
3. **Metadata** — small mono or restrained sans for author, year, chapter, or series.

Do not use an unmodified system-default font stack as the finished design. Select fonts with licensed Chinese glyph coverage and test actual rendered characters.

Use:

- strong contrast between display and caption weights;
- generous line height and slightly increased tracking on dark footage;
- warm ivory rather than pure white;
- muted antique gold only for a meaningful keyword;
- subtle shadow or localized backing only when the image requires it.

Avoid:

- tiny title bars that resemble watermarks;
- bold white captions with generic black stroke;
- highlighting several unrelated words in one sentence;
- placing every text block on the vertical centerline;
- long subtitle lines wider than a comfortable phone reading span.

## Upgrade the imagery

Make every image book-specific. Research setting, era, weather, architecture, clothing, landscape, and recurring motifs before sourcing or generating.

Prefer a visual sequence with controlled variety:

- establishing landscape;
- architectural or period detail;
- person in environment;
- symbolic close-up;
- one illustration or dreamlike image for an internal idea;
- return to a wide image for closure.

Use a consistent grade across mixed sources:

- gently lifted blacks, not crushed black;
- controlled highlights and visible cloud detail;
- restrained saturation;
- warm paper-toned neutrals;
- fine grain with no heavy vignette;
- consistent direction and softness of light.

Reject generic travel footage that could illustrate any book. A beautiful image without narrative specificity is still weak.

## Composition

- Reserve platform-safe zones before choosing crops.
- Place subjects off-center when captions need the center or lower-middle zone.
- Alternate wide, medium, and detail framing while keeping the grade coherent.
- Use negative space intentionally for title or captions.
- Keep the main focal point separate from the subtitle block.
- Test every frame at phone size.

## Motion and transitions

Use slow Ken Burns movement with one dominant vector per shot: push, drift, or slight parallax. Keep scale changes subtle, usually around 1.00→1.04 or 1.06 over the full shot.

Change shots on semantic turns or audible pauses. A typical calm sequence may hold 4–8 seconds, but shorten the opening when retention requires it.

Use one primary transition for continuity:

- soft dissolve;
- focus pull;
- gentle vertical page push.

Use at most one accent transition for a major thematic turn. Avoid decorative wipes, bounce, fast zooms, and transitions that advertise the editor.

## Cover

Do not reuse a random interior frame as the publishing cover. Create a separate cover with:

- one highly specific landscape, object, or character image;
- a large book title;
- one short emotional or curiosity hook;
- an optional small author label;
- stronger contrast than interior scenes while remaining in the same grade.

The cover must remain identifiable as a thumbnail and must not reproduce a commercial edition.

## Music

Use sparse piano, warm drone, soft strings, restrained acoustic texture, or subtle environmental sound. Avoid a prominent melody competing with narration.

Begin with the guidance in `music-mix.md`: speech foreground, music roughly 12–20 dB lower as a first pass, clean fades, and additional ducking for quiet lines.

## Review questions

Before approval, ask:

1. Could this image belong only to this book, or to any inspirational video?
2. Does the title feel like designed identity rather than a watermark?
3. Are black areas preserving detail?
4. Does every highlighted word deserve emphasis?
5. Do shot scale and composition change enough to sustain attention?
6. Is the cover stronger and clearer than an interior frame?
7. Can narration be understood easily on phone speakers with music present?
