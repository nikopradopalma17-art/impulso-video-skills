# Background music and mix

## Select music

Match the narration, not merely the genre label:

- reflective literature: sparse piano, soft strings, warm drone, brushed texture;
- historical or expansive stories: restrained acoustic or orchestral atmosphere;
- mystery or tension: low pulse, minimal percussion, controlled dissonance;
- warm cartoon or children’s books: light acoustic motifs without busy melody;
- nonfiction: neutral ambient rhythm with limited harmonic movement.

Prefer user-owned, public-domain, properly licensed, platform-cleared, or newly generated music. Record the source and license. Do not assume a track is safe because it is available online.

## Mix

- Keep narration as the clear foreground.
- Start the music bed around 12–20 dB below the perceived narration level as a practical first pass; adjust by ear and meters.
- Use sidechain-style ducking or automation when music competes with consonants.
- Reduce music further beneath quiet or emotionally important lines.
- Avoid strong vocals, prominent lead melodies, or dense percussion under speech.
- Fade in over roughly 0.5–1.5 seconds and fade out over roughly 1–2 seconds unless the reference calls for another shape.
- Keep peaks below 0 dBFS and leave sensible encoding headroom.

Measure the final mixed MP4, not only the source tracks. Check speech intelligibility on phone speakers and at low playback volume. A quiet track that disappears entirely is also a failed mix; it should be felt without demanding attention.
