# Visual assets and publishing cover

## Choose the image language

Derive four fields before sourcing or generating:

1. **World** — country, region, period, weather, architecture, landscape, and material culture.
2. **Emotional register** — warm, lonely, tense, comic, mysterious, expansive, intimate, or documentary.
3. **Representation** — vintage landscape, archival collage, symbolic still life, painterly illustration, clean cartoon, or mixed media.
4. **Continuity anchors** — palette, grain, character design, camera distance, border treatment, and light direction.

Use:

- literary classics and historical fiction: vintage scenery, period objects, maps, archival paper, restrained illustration;
- fantasy and magical realism: ordinary environments with one controlled impossible detail;
- children’s or humorous books: coherent cartoon characters and simplified environments;
- nonfiction: documentary imagery, diagrams, objects, places, or author context;
- psychology and philosophy: concrete everyday scenes first, symbolic images second.

Do not infer culture from stereotypes. Research the setting and era before prompting or sourcing.

## Build the asset plan

For each scene record:

- timestamp and narration phrase;
- visual subject and narrative function;
- source or generation method;
- orientation and crop;
- continuity anchors;
- text-safe area;
- license or permission status.

Use images as evidence, atmosphere, or metaphor. Avoid generic filler and avoid repeating the same image with only a zoom change unless repetition has meaning.

## Generate coherent illustrations

When image generation is available:

- define a reusable style block without naming a living artist;
- repeat character age, face, hair, clothing, proportions, and color palette;
- state exact setting, era, weather, lighting, lens distance, and 9:16 composition;
- reserve negative space for captions where needed;
- generate the cover separately from scene art;
- inspect hands, text, cultural details, and continuity before use.

Generate text-free imagery when reliable Chinese typography will be added in the video compositor.

## Design the publishing cover

Create a separate 1080×1920 cover for Douyin by default.

Hierarchy:

1. book title;
2. one short hook of about 8–16 Chinese characters;
3. one unmistakable focal image;
4. optional small author or series label.

Keep the main title and focal image inside the central safe region. Assume the platform may overlay controls at the right and bottom. Test the cover at approximately 180×320 pixels; if the title or subject is unclear, simplify it.

Do not reproduce a commercial edition’s cover. An original abstract book object may appear inside the video when the user has not supplied a licensed cover.
