# Chinese narration style

## Drafting rules

- Write as a thoughtful reader speaking to another person.
- Lead with a specific tension, observation, scene, or question.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Use short and medium sentences with natural variation.
- Explain one useful idea well instead of listing many themes.
- Attribute opinions and preserve the boundary between the book and interpretation.
- Keep calls to action light and optional.

## Avoid

- habitual “不是……而是……” framing;
- stacked parallel clauses and slogan-like escalation;
- “这本书告诉我们”“真正高级的人”“所有人都应该”等 empty authority;
- fabricated quotations or quotation marks around paraphrases;
- condescension, moral judgment, and exaggerated promises;
- generic hooks unrelated to the book;
- repeating the narration verbatim as screen copy.

Do not ban a construction mechanically when it is genuinely the clearest sentence. Audit repetition and artificial rhythm instead.

## Final audit

Check:

1. Is every factual claim supported?
2. Can a vague sentence be replaced by a specific one?
3. Does the hook earn attention without misleading?
4. Is any interpretation presented as the author’s exact view?
5. Do three consecutive sentences have suspiciously identical rhythm?
6. Can any inflated adjective be removed?
7. Does the text sound natural when read aloud?
8. Does it fit the target duration at the chosen speaking rate?

Return the final script, estimated duration, source mapping, and any uncertain language.
