# Dialogue And Sound

Use this when source clips have original dialogue, sync sound, or the user asks for stronger narrative.

## Dialogue Decision Test

A dialogue line belongs only if it does at least one job:

- Creates mystery: a question, warning, secret, or unfinished thought.
- Clarifies stakes: danger, desire, deadline, conflict, promise.
- Humanizes the cut: emotional breath before action or brand reveal.
- Sets up a beat: line ends right before a hit, drop, title, or visual shock.
- Bridges unrelated visuals into a coherent story.

Remove the line if:

- The audio is muddy and cannot be understood quickly.
- It explains what the image already says.
- It breaks tone or feels pasted on.
- It fights a dense music section.

## Placement Patterns

- **Cold open line**: one sentence over black or a still shot, then first visual hit.
- **Pre-drop line**: short phrase ending 0.2-0.6 seconds before the music impact.
- **Memory line**: quiet line under slow motion or an emotional face.
- **Button line**: final phrase just before title/logo card.

## Mix Guidelines

- Keep dialogue lines usually `1.0-3.0s`.
- Use only 1-4 dialogue moments in a 40-second trailer unless the request is dialogue-led.
- Duck BGM 6-12 dB under dialogue.
- Avoid placing dialogue on top of loud percussion or vocal-heavy music.
- If using source sound effects, let them punctuate motion: sword, door, hit, engine, breath, impact.

## Subtitles

Use subtitles only when comprehension would otherwise fail. Keep them cinematic:

- One line at a time.
- Large enough to read on mobile.
- Minimal wording.
- Do not cover faces, hands, product details, or logos.
