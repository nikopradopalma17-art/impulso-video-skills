# Cinematic Trailer Playbook

Use this when the request is a movie-grade trailer, high-end promo, course/student-work reel, product launch film, or narrative montage.

## Editorial Intent

A trailer should feel authored. It is not a collection of all good shots; it is a pressure curve.

Start with one of these spines:

- **Mystery**: ask a question, withhold the answer, reveal the title.
- **Transformation**: before, process, breakthrough, invitation.
- **Threat**: calm, disturbance, escalation, impact, title.
- **Craft**: material, method, result, brand promise.
- **Spectacle**: world, motion, contrast, climax, card.

## Selection Rules

Prefer:

- Faces, eyes, hands, weapons, vehicles, locations, screens, products, or moments with readable intent.
- Shots with strong silhouettes, motion direction, foreground/background depth, or clear emotional charge.
- Fewer shots that can breathe over many shots that only prove coverage exists.

Reject:

- Repeated angles or nearly identical source time ranges.
- Low-resolution or visibly compressed material when stronger alternatives exist.
- Tone-breaking cute/comedy/demo footage in a serious cinematic brief.
- Accidental product-looking details or objects the user has explicitly disliked.
- Dialogue that does not connect to surrounding visuals.

## Pacing Patterns

For `35-45s`:

- Hook: 1-3 shots, 2.0-4.5s total.
- Premise: 3-5 shots, 7-10s total.
- Escalation: 6-10 shots, 10-14s total.
- Climax: 5-8 shots, 6-9s total.
- Title/logo: 2.5-4s hold.

For `60s`:

- Add one more phrase or chapter, but do not dilute the climax. If quality drops, cut shorter.

For `15-25s`:

- Skip exposition. Build a beat grid first and place the best shots on musical accents.

## Title And Logo Endings

- Land the logo/title on a clean music hit.
- Hold long enough to read; 2.5-3.5 seconds is usually enough.
- Keep the card clean: no extra clutter, no unnecessary animation, no small paragraphs.
- If the logo asset has a strong original color, use it directly rather than over-designing it.

## Revision Heuristics

If the cut feels chaotic:

- Remove 20-30% of shots.
- Group shots by visual logic: face to action, wide to detail, question to answer.
- Add one held shot before the climax.
- Reduce text cards.

If the cut feels slow:

- Shorten the premise section.
- Place more cuts on music hits.
- Use stronger motion changes at transitions.
- Move the title earlier only if the brand needs faster recognition.
